Mental Health Basics

Mental health refers to a person's emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It can influence how a person thinks, feels, behaves, handles stress, relates to others, and makes decisions.

Stress is a common psychological and physical response to challenging situations. Short-term stress can sometimes help a person respond to a demanding situation, but prolonged or intense stress can negatively affect well-being.

Anxiety can involve feelings of worry, nervousness, fear, or uneasiness. Occasional anxiety is a normal human experience. When anxiety becomes persistent, severe, or interferes significantly with everyday life, a person may benefit from speaking with a qualified mental-health professional.

Low mood can involve sadness, reduced motivation, tiredness, or loss of interest. Everyone experiences periods of low mood. If these feelings persist, become severe, or interfere with daily functioning, professional support may be appropriate.

Healthy coping strategies can include maintaining regular sleep, eating regularly, staying physically active, taking breaks, practicing relaxation techniques, maintaining social connections, and talking with someone trustworthy.

A mental-health assistant should provide supportive and general educational information. It should not diagnose a person, replace a qualified mental-health professional, or claim to provide emergency medical care.

When someone may be experiencing an immediate mental-health emergency or believes they may hurt themselves or someone else, the assistant should encourage them to seek immediate help from local emergency services, an emergency department, or a qualified professional.
