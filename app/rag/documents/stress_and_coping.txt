STRESS AND COPING

WHAT IS STRESS?

Stress is a natural response to challenging, demanding, or difficult situations. It can involve feelings of worry, tension, irritability, or being overwhelmed. Stress can affect both the mind and body.

People may experience stress because of studies, work, relationships, family responsibilities, financial concerns, health concerns, or major life changes.

COMMON EXPERIENCES ASSOCIATED WITH STRESS

Stress can affect people in different ways. Common experiences may include:

- Feeling worried, tense, overwhelmed, or irritable.
- Difficulty relaxing.
- Difficulty concentrating.
- Trouble sleeping.
- Headaches, body pains, or stomach discomfort.
- Feeling tired or having less energy.

These experiences alone should not be used to diagnose a mental-health condition.

GENERAL COPING STRATEGIES

Some general strategies that may help with everyday stress include:

- Break large or demanding tasks into smaller, manageable steps.
- Set realistic priorities and focus on one task at a time.
- Take regular breaks during demanding work or study sessions.
- Maintain a regular sleep routine when possible.
- Include physical activity in daily life when appropriate.
- Use slow breathing or other relaxation practices.
- Talk with a trusted friend, family member, or support person.
- Spend time on enjoyable or relaxing activities.
- Eat regular, balanced meals and stay hydrated.
- Reduce unnecessary sources of pressure where possible.

SLEEP AND STRESS

Stress can make it more difficult to relax and sleep. Good sleep habits may support both physical and mental well-being.

Helpful habits can include maintaining a regular sleep schedule, creating a quiet and comfortable sleep environment, reducing screen use before bedtime, and allowing time to relax before going to sleep.

SOCIAL SUPPORT

Staying connected with people you trust can provide emotional and practical support. Sharing concerns and feelings with a trusted person may help someone feel less isolated.

WHEN TO SEEK ADDITIONAL SUPPORT

Consider seeking support from a qualified professional when emotional difficulties are persistent, severe, distressing, or significantly interfere with daily life, studies, work, relationships, or usual activities.

This document provides general educational information. It is not intended to diagnose a mental-health condition or replace professional care.
